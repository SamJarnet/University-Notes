# Social Engineering

#### Definition:
- Social engineering concerns techniques to psychologically manipulate people into performing some action or divulging specific information

#### Why does it work?
- People are pretty retarded
- And this can't be fixed
	- Just ask for their password
- 33% of security incidents are social engineering

#### Anatomy of SE attacks:
- Social engineer
- Target
- Compliance Principles
- Techniques
- Goal 
- Medium

#### Information Gathering:

- On the web:
	- Company website
		- Background
		- Emails
	- Social networks
- Dumpster diving:
	- Employees commonly use their office thrash rather than a shredder
	- Attacker may find information
- Shoulder surfing
	- Watch someone enter their password and steal it 

#### Interactions with target:
-  Phishing:
	- Practice of sending emails appearing to be from reputable sources with the goal of influencing or gaining personal information
	- URL manipulation yuotube.com
- Vishing and Smishing:
	- Social engineering using
		- Telephones
		- Texts
	- Tools:
		- Caller ID spoofer
- Physical Impersonation:
	- The attacker shows up in person posing as someone not suspicious
		- Maintenance
		- Check alarms/smoke detectors
		- Deliver a package/food

#### Baiting:
- Like real-world Trojan horse:
- Examples:
	- Infected removable media left where people can find them
		- Car park
		- Bathroom
		- Meeting room
		- Elevator
	- A gift with a wiretap inside: photo album, a USB hub

#### Tailgating:
- Accessing a secured building/area without any smart-card/biometric, by simply walking closely behind an authorised employee
- General strategy:
	- Wait until someone is entering and follow them
	- They are likely to allow them in due to politeness or fake card/show to be searching for it
