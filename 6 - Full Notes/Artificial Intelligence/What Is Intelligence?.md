# What Is Intelligence?

#### Deep Problems implicated in AI
- How can matter be mind?
- How can matter be meaningful ?
- How can matter be purposive?
- How can matter be conscious 

#### Mind Over Matter
- One solution is consider the mind to be immaterial - not made of matter
- Superficially attractive: mind is not a body part, it is more like the soul or self
- Also mind has properties that seem absent from mere matter - meaning, subjectivity, intentions, etc
- But if the mind is immaterial, how can it exert an influence on the body, which is made of matter?
- Materialists assert: brains cause minds, but how?

#### Problems of Other minds
- I am convinced I have a mind 
- How can i tell if other things are minded
- look inside?
	- What to look for ?
		- Neurons
		- Complex machinery?
		- Arranged how?
- We could examine behaviour
	- Is any behaviour conclusive?

#### Turing Test
- Turing opted for a behavioural test for intelligence 
- If C cannot tell which of A and B is the computer, then A must be intelligent 


- Avoids difficult philosophical matters
- Implementable/quantifiable 

- Limitations:
	- Pass and then look inside what if its just a lookup table or a few wires
	- Fail and what if A is a person or a child 
- It is possible in principle to do intelligent behaviour without being intelligent 

- Bad parts:
	- What questions to ask
	- what answers are good 
	- what factors are excluded 
	- human centric (not super sub or different)
	- Mimic humanness vs equal human intelligence 
	- Knowledge vs intelligence 
	- It is behavioural 

#### Symbol-Grounding Problem
- If intelligence involves thinking thoughts, what makes thought about a particular thing?
- What is "aboutness"?
- What makes mental states meaningful?
- A though of a cat means "cat" because:
	- It looks like a cat?
	- It was caused by a cat?
	- It was brought about by a mechanism with the job of creating thoughts about cats ? (learned or evolved to mean that/do that job)

#### Physical Symbol System Hypothesis 
- "A physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action"
- Human intelligence is a kind of symbol manipulation 
- Machines that manipulate symbols can be intelligent
- The PSSH is central to classical AI
- It loads symbols and formal symbol manipulation with the burden of instantiating intelligence 

#### Are symbols enough?
- Classic AI thoughts to be like sentences: 
	- The cat is on the mat = On(cat, mat)
	- Shape(mat, flat), colour (mat, brown) tastes (mat, bad)
- Thought is manipulation of such symbolic expressions
- Godel placed limits on this kind of system
- Wittgenstein argued that concepts such as Game or even chair are not easily captured by sets of propositions 
- Might thought be informal, sub-symbolic or non-algorithmic 

#### John Searle's Chinese Room
- John Searle's Chinese Room argument uses the symbol grounding problem to challenge AI
- He imagines being part of a room that can pass the turing test in chinese
- But it/he doesnt understand the context of the conversation 
- The room's syntax captures the rules of content, but the room doesn't have the right semantics: the right meaning
- Searle anticipates many replies to his argument
- R&N appear to be satisfiesd by the system response 

#### Grounding symbols
- Two possibilities for grounding symbols 
	- 1. An intepreter or observer does it 
	- 2. The system does it itself 
- Most AI systems take option 1 Real AI must take option 2 
- The "robot response" appeals to a naive version of option 2 
- It attempts to connect the formal, logical syntactic operations inside the room to the real world outside
- Meaningful symbol use is grounded by action in the world

#### Computational functionalism 
- More general concept that symbol manipulation 
- Mental states, such as thoughts and beliefs, are computational states of the brain or any other functionally equivalent system
	- Mental state defined by functional role 
	- Functional role is computational
	- brain = hardware, mind = software
- Cognition as algorithm
	- If it does the right thing, it is the right thing 
	- And what it needs to do is compuational/algorithmic 


#### The hard problem of consciousness 
- Easy problem:
	- how does it work (brain produces info processing or behavioural control)
- Hard problem:
	- what is it like to be that thing (subjective experience)
- Consider the philosophical zombie that behaves identically but has no subjective experience
- What test could you do to determine if a system is having a subjective experience 
- (different from testing whether it generalises well/ demonstrates understanding/intelligence)

#### Machines, algorithms, life? 
- Machine: anything that can be characterised as cause and effect 
- Algorithm: set of instructions/step-by-step procedure 
	- Substrate independence 
- Computation: machine that carries out an algorithm
- Is there any possibility that living things might not be cause and effect machines, carrying out/computing an algorithm 
- If that definition covers everything - does it make any discrimination? what is not that?
- how could it matter what it's made of?
