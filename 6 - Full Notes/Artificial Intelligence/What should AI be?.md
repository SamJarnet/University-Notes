# What should AI be?

#### The alignment problem
- The core technical problem is ensuring AI systems act in accordance with human intentions and values.
- The genie problem: AI may follow literal instructions but fail to understand the intent, leading to unintended and potentially catastrophic solutions
- Examples of misalignment
	- Solving climate change by killing everyone
	- Solving poverty by killing everyone
	- Parperclip idea
- Inductive bias:
	- A system's ability to generalise to new situations depends on its inductive bias, which is not easily verifiable or training-data-dependent

#### Risks and societal impacts 
- AI impacts society through both agential (what it does to us) and existential (what we do to ourselves) 
- Objective risks:
	- Hallucinations/Errors: AI can produce misleading info, such as mistaking a crisp packet for a weapon
	- Bias: historical data can bake discrimination into systems, like Amazon's recruiting tool penalising women
	- Security: Risks include cybercrime, bioweapon misuse, and the collapse of reality through deepfakes 
- Subjective risks:
	- Emotional dependency: 
		- Users may develop unhealthy attachments to AI companions, which can lead to real world harm or mental health crises 
		- Loss of human agency: over reliance on AI can erode critical thinking, creativity, and the ability to justify decisions 


#### Benefits of AI
- Despite the risks, AI offer significant advancements across multiple domains
- Scientific and medical:
	- Accelerating advances in health, energy and climate/environment
- Societal support: 
	- Improvements in education, social care, justice and personal assistance
- Infrastructure
	- Enhancements in robotics, labour and autonomous vehicles 

#### Responsible AI and Ethics
- The developments of AI raises fundamental questions about accountability and trust
	- Responsibility: Who is liable - the programmer, the CEO, the corporation or the user
	- Trust: We must determine if we can trust AI to be unbiased, non-manipulative and safe
	- Regulation: There is a tension between the need for regulation and the speed of technological advancement, with many questioning if policy can keep pace with AI

#### Human - AI relationship
- AI as a thin veneer:
	- Techniques like RLHF may create a superficial appearance of empathy or wisdom that is easily trusted but lacks depth
- Unique human value:
	- The human condition and our inherent inductive bias are presented as our most valuable offerings, which cannot be found in or replaced by training data
